Phone mount for the telescope

Zenigo finds where your telescope points by photographing the sky with the phone camera. That only works if the phone sits on the telescope firmly and always in the same place. This page shows how to build such a mount from a few standard parts and two 3D-printed pieces, which you can adjust to your telescope and download right here.

Why a rail in the finder shoe

A phone strapped to the tube with a bike or tripod clamp works, but it shifts a little whenever you push the telescope, and after every setup it sits somewhere else. Each time, the eyepiece point no longer fits and has to be set again.

Almost every telescope already has a precise, sturdy mount for small accessories: the finder shoe. A printed adapter slides into it like a finder and carries a short Picatinny rail, the rail system known from riflescopes and camera rigs. On this rail you clamp a phone holder and, if you like, a mirror:

  • Rigid: the shoe and the rail clamp with screws, nothing wobbles when you move the telescope.
  • Repeatable: the rail has a slot every 10 mm. Everything goes back into the same place, so the eyepiece point stays valid from night to night.
  • Quick: the adapter slides out of the shoe with the phone holder still on it. Nothing to strap or tape to the tube.
  • Expandable: the rail takes anything with a Picatinny clamp or a 1/4" camera thread: a red light, a dew heater controller, a second camera.

The idea with the mirror

The camera on the back of the phone has to look along the telescope. Mounted directly, the phone stands upright on the tube, and its screen faces the back of the telescope, hard to see from the eyepiece and nearly impossible to reach when the telescope points high.

With a 45° mirror in front of the camera, the phone can lie flat on the rail, screen up, with its top edge towards the opening of the tube. The camera looks down into the mirror, and the mirror turns its view by 90° along the tube. You read and tap the phone like a small control panel next to the eyepiece.

A single mirror mirrors the image. Zenigo flips it back: choose Settings → Telescope → Pointing → Top edge, and when you start the camera the app asks whether the image is mirrored; answer Mirrored image on. (The switch is also in Settings → Camera.) Without the mirror, keep the phone upright and use the normal Camera pointing.

Use a front-surface mirror. An ordinary mirror reflects twice, at the glass and at the silvering behind it, and every star turns into a double star.

Building it

  1. Print the adapter

    Choose your shoe type below, set how far the rail may reach forward and back, and download the STL. Print it as it comes, slot face on the bed: no supports needed. PETG or ASA hold up better than PLA in the cold and in a hot car; 4 walls and 20–30 % infill are plenty.

    Vixen/Synta shoes differ by a few tenths of a millimetre. Measure the slot of yours, adjust the dovetail and print a short test piece first (foot length 20 mm, no overhang).

  2. Screw the rail onto the adapter

    Slide the T-nuts of the rail into the open slot of the adapter, put the rail on top and screw it down. The hole spacing of the rail doesn’t matter, the nuts go wherever the holes are. Let the rail reach towards the opening of the tube.

  3. Mount the phone holder

    Clamp a camera mount onto the rail and screw the phone holder onto it. Lock it with the knurled ring so it can’t turn. Without a mirror, the phone stands upright and its camera looks towards the opening; that’s all you need.

  4. Add the mirror (optional)

    Glue the front-surface mirror flat onto the 45° face of the printed holder, centred. Screw the holder onto the second camera mount, its mirror face towards the opening, and clamp it in front of the phone holder. The phone lies flat, top edge towards the opening, with the camera above the middle of the mirror: the mirror centre sits about 18 mm behind the front edge of the holder and 30 mm above its base. Turn the mirror exactly along the rail, then lock the ring.

  5. Set up Zenigo

    With the mirror: Settings → Telescope → Pointing → Top edge, and answer Mirrored image on when the camera starts. Then locate once and set the eyepiece point on a bright star. As long as nothing moves on the rail, it stays valid for the next nights too.

A long rail is a lever on the small clamping screw of the shoe. If it wobbles, add a strap around the tube at the far end of the rail.
