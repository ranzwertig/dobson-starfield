Features

A phone on a telescope you move by hand can tell where its camera points to a fraction of a degree, and from there where the telescope points, without encoders. Zenigo builds the rest around that: what to look at tonight, how to get there, and what you saw. Without a telescope, the same app is a star finder.

01 — The core

Plate solving

The position comes from the stars

Locate averages about a second of camera frames, picks out the stars and matches their pattern against a catalogue of 5,000 stars. The result is accurate to a fraction of a degree and doesn’t care how far the compass is off next to a metal tube. Each green circle in the picture is a star it recognised.

  • After the first solve, the gyroscope carries the position while you push. Continuous keeps checking it against the stars about twice a second; with the last position known, the match itself takes milliseconds.
  • The phone never sits exactly parallel to the tube. Centre a star in the eyepiece, tap Locate, then tap that star on the screen: from then on the crosshair marks where the telescope points, not the camera, until the holder moves.
  • For fainter stars than the video stream can show, take a night photo with the phone’s camera app. In night mode it can expose for several seconds, and Zenigo solves the photo just the same.

Push-to

Directions you can push by

The two numbers at the top tell you how far to turn and tilt the telescope. A Dobson or any alt-az mount moves along exactly these two axes. They count down while you push, and the target pill turns green when the target is in the eyepiece.

  • Rings around the crosshair show your finder and the true field of the eyepiece in use, calculated from the telescope and eyepieces you entered.
  • The round eyepiece view Pro shows the stars around where the telescope points, with the target, or an arrow towards it. It is turned and mirrored the way your Newtonian or star diagonal shows them, so the pattern matches what you see.

Choosing targets

Objects for your telescope, not for any telescope

Zenigo knows your aperture, your eyepieces and how low and high your mount can point. Its categories use all of that.

  • My telescope keeps objects that are bright enough for your aperture and not far larger than the field of your widest eyepiece. For galaxies and nebulae it judges the surface brightness, which is what makes a large galaxy of magnitude 9 harder than a small one.
  • Good now lists what is high enough and in reach of your mount right now, and leaves out galaxies and nebulae next to a bright Moon. In reach leaves out what never comes into your mount’s range from your site.
  • Messier, Caldwell and Herschel 400 can be combined with these. There are about 2,000 deep-sky objects in total, and stars down to magnitude 9 appear as you zoom in.

Planning

Tonight, the plan and the logbook

  • Tonight shows when it gets dark, how long it stays dark without the Moon, which planets are up and the best deep-sky objects of the night.
  • The planned list puts first what sets first, and Next takes you to the next one that is well placed. Starred keeps what you want to see some day, with one tap.
  • Mark an object Seen and the logbook fills in time, instrument, eyepiece, altitude and Moon. You add seeing, transparency and a note.

Live connection Pro

Phone on the tube, tablet in your hand

A phone in its holder near the top of the tube is hard to read. Connect a second phone or tablet and it shows where the telescope points: the crosshair, the arrow and the directions. A target you choose on either device becomes the target on both. If you like, the tablet also shows the telescope’s camera image, with the stars and rings drawn on it.

The two devices pair by scanning each other’s QR code and then talk directly over your Wi-Fi or a phone’s hotspot. No server and no internet are involved, so it works at a dark site too. Only the device on the telescope needs Pro; anyone can follow it.

Your own photos

What is in that photo, labelled

Open a sky photo from your gallery and Zenigo finds where it was pointing from its stars, then draws on it what is there: constellations, star names, the galaxies, nebulae and clusters at their true size, and the planets and the Moon where they were when you took it. The time comes from the photo itself.

  • Clean, Labelled or Atlas with the constellation figures and a coordinate grid, and as many labels as you like.
  • In this photo lists every object with a cut-out of your picture, marks the ones not in your logbook yet, and logs them for the night you took it.
  • Save or share a labelled copy with a caption. Your location stays out of it unless you add it.

02 — More in Zenigo

For planning, more sky, and more devices

Star finder

Hold the phone up and see what is there, with or without the camera. No telescope needed.

Sound guidance

Don’t look at the screen: clicks for turning, chirps for tilting, a chord when the target is in the field.

At a dark site

Red light for the whole app. Added to the home screen it needs no signal: catalogue, solver, planning and logbook run on the phone.

Object details + Pro

The night bar, why an object suits your telescope, the Moon’s craters on the terminator, Jupiter’s moons. Pro picks the best of your eyepieces.

Observing programmes + Pro

Messier with your progress. Pro adds Caldwell, Herschel 400 and II, Binocular Messier and the 110 Best of the NGC.

Sync Pro

Lists, logbook and telescope settings on phone, tablet and computer, end-to-end encrypted. No account.

Coming up Pro

Eclipses, close pairs of the Moon and planets, oppositions, and meteor showers with how much the Moon disturbs them.

ISS transits Pro

When the ISS or Tiangong crosses the Moon or the Sun near you, to a tenth of a second, with a countdown at the telescope.

Satellites Pro

The ISS and bright satellites on the map, and their next visible passes.

Your own horizon Pro

Record the roofs and trees around your spot once; Zenigo shows what is really free and when it comes out. Several locations.

Short route Pro

Your plan in the order with the least turning for your mount, from where the telescope points now.

Your own lists Pro

As many as you like, for a season, a club night or the kids, with what is visible tonight first.

Several instruments Pro

Each telescope with its own eyepieces, finder and eyepiece point, and binoculars. One tap to switch.

Extended catalogue Pro

All of NGC and IC, about 10,000 more objects, and stars to magnitude 11. Downloaded once, then offline.

Logbook statistics Pro

What you have seen by month, kind of object and constellation, and your year in review to share.
