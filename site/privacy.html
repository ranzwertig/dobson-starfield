Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

Zenigo is built so that your data stays on your device. The app works entirely in your browser: camera images, your location and motion sensor data are processed locally and are never sent to us or to anyone else (the optional live connection sends the camera image, if you turn that on, only directly to your own second device in the same network). This policy explains the little data that is processed anyway.

What the app processes on your device

Camera and photos

When you start the camera or choose a photo, the image is analysed in your browser to find the stars in it. Images are not stored and not uploaded. The camera only runs while you have it switched on, and only after you have allowed access.

For a photo from your gallery, the app also reads the time and, if the photo contains it, the place it was taken from the photo’s EXIF data, to show the planets where they were. They are used while the photo is open and not stored. A labelled copy you save or share is a new image made on your device; it contains no EXIF or GPS data, and the place appears in its caption only if you switch that on.

Location

The app needs your approximate position to convert star positions into directions for your telescope. You can enter it by hand or let the browser determine it (the “GPS” button in Settings), which asks for your permission first. The position is stored on your device, and in your sync group if you turn on sync between devices.

Motion sensors and compass

If you turn on “Align”, the app reads the orientation sensors of your device to move the map with the phone. This data is processed in the moment and not stored, except for a compass correction you choose to save.

Observing list and logbook

Your observing list and logbook entries (object, date and time, eyepiece, altitude, Moon, the coordinates of your site and your notes) are stored in your browser’s local storage. They are only uploaded if you turn on sync between devices, and then only end-to-end encrypted. An export creates a file on your device; what happens with it is up to you.

Satellites

When you are online and satellites are switched on in the map filter, the app loads the current orbital elements of the ISS and other bright satellites from our own server (zenigo.app), which gets them from CelesTrak (celestrak.org). No location or other personal data is sent with this request; it is an ordinary page request handled by Cloudflare as described under Hosting. The positions are calculated on your device. The orbital elements are not stored, so satellites are not shown offline.

Settings stored on your device

Your settings (location, pointing mode, chosen camera, camera field of view, eyepiece point, compass correction, map filter, chosen target and display options) are saved in your browser’s local storage, so they are still there next time. For offline use the browser also keeps a copy of the app (service worker cache). This data stays on your device, except for the settings that sync between your devices if you turn on sync. You can delete it at any time by clearing the website data for Zenigo in your browser settings.

Sync between devices (optional)

Sync is off until you turn it on (Menu › Sync with other devices, or Settings › Sync). It keeps your observing list, starred objects, logbook and the telescope settings that travel with you (location, mount, map filter, focal length, eyepieces, Barlow and finder) the same on your devices. Eyepiece point, pointing, camera, compass correction and display settings stay on each device.

  • End-to-end encrypted: setting up sync creates a random sync code on your device. The data is encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) with a key derived from this code before it is sent. The code and the key never reach our server, so we cannot read your synced data. The code is passed from device to device as a QR code or a link; the code is in the part of the link after “#”, which browsers do not send to any server.
  • What our server stores per sync group: the encrypted copy, a random identifier derived from the code, a hash of an access password derived from the code, the size, and when the group was created and last changed. No name, no email address, no account.
  • Where: in a Cloudflare D1 database located in the European Union, as part of the hosting described above.
  • Connection data: each sync is a request to our server, processed as described under Hosting (IP address and so on). The server keeps no log of sync requests; to limit misuse, Cloudflare counts requests per IP address for a minute without storing them.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR: you turn sync on to use this service. Storing the sync code on your device is necessary for the service you asked for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG); no consent banner is needed, and sync sets no cookies.
  • How long: until you delete it (Settings › Sync › Delete from the server, or a new sync code, which deletes the old copy). Groups that have not been changed for more than 12 months may be deleted by us.
  • Your rights: you can delete the synced copy yourself at any time. Without your sync code we cannot tell which encrypted copy is yours, so we cannot give access to it or delete it on request (Art. 11 GDPR).

Buying Zenigo Pro

Zenigo Pro is sold by Paddle.com Market Ltd, Judd House, 18–29 Mora Street, London EC1V 8BT, United Kingdom, as merchant of record. When you buy Pro, you enter your name, email address, country and payment details in Paddle’s checkout. Paddle processes them as an independent controller, to carry out the purchase, issue the invoice and meet its tax obligations. Its privacy policy applies.

  • The Pro page: zenigo.app/pro loads Paddle’s script (Paddle.js) from Paddle’s servers to show the price for your country and open the checkout. Your browser then connects to Paddle, which receives your IP address and browser details and may set cookies for the checkout and fraud prevention. No other page of Zenigo, and not the app, loads anything from Paddle. To show the right price, our server tells the page the country Cloudflare derives from your IP address; it is not stored.
  • What we receive: after the purchase, Paddle notifies our server of it. We receive the order number, the product, the date and the country, for our accounting. We do not store your email address or payment details. Our server uses the order number to create a licence key and keeps the order number, the key, its identifier and its dates, so that the key can be shown again for the order, deactivated after a refund or blocked if it is published, and so that a renewal can start when the current licence ends. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of the contract) and, for the accounting records, Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR. Retention: for as long as the licence exists, and beyond that for the statutory retention periods of up to 10 years.
  • The licence key on your devices: it contains the order number, the purchase date, the end of the licence period and a digital signature, but no name or email address. Zenigo checks the signature and the end date on your device, without contacting a server. The key is stored in your browser on the device. After buying, the welcome page fetches the key for your order from our server with the order number; nothing else is sent.
  • Sync with Pro: when your device syncs, it also sends the licence key, so that our server can check that Pro is active. The key is checked and not stored with your sync data. When the licence period ends, the server stops accepting syncs for this key; the encrypted copy stays until you delete it, and like every sync group it may be deleted by us after 12 months without changes.

No cookies, no tracking

Zenigo sets no cookies, uses no analytics or advertising services and has no social media plugins. Fonts are delivered from our own server, not from third-party providers. The only exception is Paddle’s checkout on the Pro page, described under Buying Zenigo Pro.

Links to other websites

The object details in the app link to external sources (SEDS Messier Database, In-The-Sky.org, NASA, SIMBAD and Wikipedia), and the menu links to location-based services (meteoblue for seeing and clouds, lightpollutionmap.info for light pollution, Heavens-Above for satellite passes). These links contain the coordinates of your location, rounded to about 100 m, so the provider can show the information for it. These sites are only contacted when you tap a link. From then on, the privacy policy of the respective provider applies.

The DIY accessories page links to products on Amazon. These are affiliate links: if you buy something through them, Zenigo receives a commission from Amazon; the price stays the same for you. The links carry a partner ID so Amazon can attribute the purchase. Amazon is only contacted when you open such a link; from then on, Amazon’s privacy policy applies.
