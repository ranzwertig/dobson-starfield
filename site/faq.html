Basics

Does it cost anything?
No. Zenigo is free, with no account and no ads, and everything you need to find objects with your telescope stays free, including labelling your night photos. Zenigo Pro is optional: a 12-month licence, paid once, with no automatic renewal, for sync between devices, the live connection to a second device, Coming up, satellites, the eyepiece view and the short route.
Is there an app in the App Store or Play Store?
No, Zenigo is a web app: open zenigo.app/app in Safari or Chrome and add it to the home screen. It then starts full screen like any other app and keeps working without a connection. When a new version is ready, the app offers to update.
Does it work without signal?
Yes. Once added to the home screen and opened online, everything runs on the phone: the star catalogue, plate solving, planning, the observing list and the logbook. Only the satellites need a connection, and sync waits until the device is online again.
What is push-to, and how is it different from GoTo?
With push-to you move the telescope by hand and a device tells you which way and how far. A GoTo mount has motors and moves by itself. Push-to with Zenigo needs no power for the mount and no alignment routine, and it keeps a Dobson as simple as it is; you do the pushing. Encoder push-to kits need power and an alignment on two stars.
Can I use it without a telescope?
Yes, as a star finder: hold the phone up with Align on and the map shows the stars, constellations and planets behind it. Point the camera at the sky and tap Locate (the first time: Just locate) to have the names checked against the stars in the image.
Can it tell me what is in my night-sky photo?
Yes. Menu → Label a night photo: Zenigo works out where it was pointing from the stars in it and draws the constellations, star names and objects on it, the planets where they were when it was taken (the time comes from the photo). You can save or share the labelled copy. It needs about ten stars and a field of at least about 10°. More in the help.
How long does the setup take?
About 15 minutes the first time: install, enter location and telescope, mount the phone, locate on a bright star and set the eyepiece point. On later nights, with the holder in the same place, it is Locate and go. Getting started walks you through it.

Telescopes and equipment

Which telescopes does it work with?
Telescopes you move by hand: Dobsonians, refractors and Newtonians on alt-az mounts, travel scopes and Maksutovs on a photo tripod, and binoculars on a mount. On equatorial mounts the map, the crosshair and the arrow work, but the “turn” and “tilt” numbers don’t match the mount’s axes. Details for each: Which telescopes work.
How do I attach the phone?
Any holder that keeps the phone steady while you push, with the camera looking roughly along the tube: a clamp around the tube, or a holder in the finder shoe. It doesn’t have to be parallel; you set the offset once with a star. With a 3D printer you can build the phone holder for the finder shoe.
Can the phone look through the eyepiece or the finder?
Not for locating. Recognising the star pattern needs a field of at least about 10°, and eyepieces and finders show far less. The camera has to see the sky directly, next to the tube. A 90° mirror or periscope in front of it is fine.
Can I use a webcam or another camera?
Yes, if the browser sees it as a camera: a USB webcam on a laptop or on an Android phone that supports USB cameras, or a DSLR or mirrorless camera through an HDMI capture stick. Mount it on the tube with its own lens and choose it in Settings → Camera → Camera. A photo from any camera can also be solved from the gallery.
Does it work without the camera?
Yes, with less accuracy. With Align on, the map follows the phone’s compass and motion sensors, and in top edge mode the top of the phone is the pointer. Expect one to a few degrees: enough with a finder or a low-power eyepiece.
Do I need to enter my eyepieces?
Not to find objects. With the telescope’s focal length and aperture and your eyepieces entered, Zenigo also shows each eyepiece’s field around the crosshair, an eyepiece view of what you should see, which objects suit your telescope, and which of your eyepieces is best for each object.

Accuracy and conditions

How accurate is it?
After a Locate, the camera’s direction is known to a fraction of a degree. In practice the limit is how well the eyepiece point is set and whether the holder stays put. With a low-power eyepiece of about one degree field, the target is in view. The motion sensor drifts a little over long moves, so Locate again near the target, or turn on Continuous.
Why not just use the compass?
Next to a metal tube a phone’s compass is often 10° or more off, and it gets worse pointing high. Zenigo uses the compass only until the first Locate; after that the position comes from the stars, and the gyroscope carries it along while you push.
Does it work from a town or under light pollution?
Usually, yes. Locate needs about ten stars in the camera image, and the brighter stars show through light pollution. Aim at a part of the sky with bright stars, raise Frames to stack, or take a night photo with the phone’s camera app, which exposes far longer.
Does it work at dusk or with the Moon up?
As soon as the brighter stars are visible, Locate works. Keep the Moon out of the camera image: its glare hides the stars around it. Before that, the map follows the compass, roughly; a compass correction from an earlier night improves it.
What if there are clouds?
Locate ignores what it can’t match, so a gap with about ten stars in it is enough. Under complete cover only the compass works.
Can it guide me to the Sun?
Yes, by compass and motion sensor, because Locate doesn’t work in daylight. Only with a certified solar filter covering the front of the telescope and the finder covered: read the safety notes.

Phone and camera

Which phones and tablets work?
Current iPhones and Android phones with Safari or Chrome, and tablets and computers with Chrome, Edge or Safari. For Locate the camera has to show about ten stars in a short exposure, which most phones from the last few years manage. A tablet makes a good second screen in your hand with the live connection.
How long does the battery last?
Turn the camera off when you don’t need it (Align keeps the position from the motion sensor), lower the brightness or use red light, and take a power bank for long nights: the camera and the screen use the most power, and cold drains batteries faster.
The camera finds no stars, although they are there
Check that the lens is clean and not fogged up, that the camera is focused, and that nothing covers part of the view. Settings → Camera → Capture has a diagnostics view that shows what Locate saw.
Can I use a tablet in my hand while the phone sits on the telescope?
Yes. The live connection pairs the two by QR codes; the tablet then shows where the telescope points and the way to the target, optionally with the telescope’s camera image. The devices talk directly over Wi-Fi or a phone’s hotspot, without a server or internet.

Data and privacy

What happens to my data?
Camera images and motion data are processed on the device and never uploaded. The observing list, logbook, settings and your location are stored in the browser on the device; they leave it only in the encrypted sync copy, if you turn sync on, and the location also when you open one of the linked weather or satellite websites from the Tonight panel. The optional camera image of the live connection goes only directly to your other device. Details: Privacy.
Can I use it on several devices?
Yes. Sync keeps the observing list, logbook and telescope settings the same on all your devices. It is end-to-end encrypted with a key from the sync code, which never reaches the server; the server only stores a locked copy.
Can I lose my logbook?
Clearing the browser’s website data deletes it from the device. Safari can also clear data of websites you haven’t opened for seven days; added to the home screen, Zenigo is exempt. Use sync, or back up now and then in Settings → General → Backup and restore.
Is there tracking or analytics?
No. No cookies, no analytics, no advertising services.

Compared with other ways

How is it different from Celestron StarSense Explorer?
StarSense Explorer works the same way, recognising the stars with the phone camera, but its app’s pointing features are unlocked by a code that comes only with StarSense Explorer telescopes, which have a dock with a mirror for the phone (Celestron). Zenigo works on the telescope you already have, with any holder. More in the comparison.
Is it as good as encoders?
Encoders measure the mount’s axes and need a two-star alignment; Zenigo measures the sky itself. Both get a target into a low-power eyepiece. Encoders don’t need a view of the sky between alignments and don’t drift; Zenigo needs no hardware on the mount. See the comparison.
Isn’t a planetarium app enough?
On its own, without encoders or a GoTo mount, a planetarium app shows the sky but doesn’t know where the telescope points, so it can only help with star hopping. Zenigo knows the telescope’s direction from the stars and gives the way from there.
